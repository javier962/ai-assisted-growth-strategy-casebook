Closing the Listings Gap

Commercial-model redesign for an automotive marketplace where listing-slot economics conflicted with inventory breadth.

If the package prices scarcity, dealers rationally keep their full stock elsewhere. Reward breadth, then monetize performance on top.

01 · Commercial paradox

Scarcity can create the listings gap

When paid packages cap inventory, dealers rationally keep their full stock on uncapped alternatives and use the capped portal as a curated shop window.

BEFORESlot scarcity

Package value is tied to a fixed number of listings.

BEHAVIORDealer rationing

Only selected vehicles are uploaded; full stock lives elsewhere.

OUTCOMEWeaker marketplace

Lower breadth reduces consumer utility and network effects.

02 · Model redesign

Separate access from acceleration

Make it rational to upload the full book, then charge for prominence, performance and ancillary value.

01Broad access

Volume bands, active-stock tiers or uncapped access.

02Visibility

Featured placement and prominence products.

03Performance

Products tied to leads, conversion or outcomes.

04Adjacency

Finance, valuation, wholesale or dealer tools.

03 · Commercial options

Different mechanics, same strategic objective

The benchmark is not one universal price model; it is the incentive created by the model.

VOLUME BANDSScale with active stock

Effective unit economics improve as dealer inventory grows.

AVERAGE STOCKMatch real inventory

Subscription tiers reflect the dealer’s actual operating base.

UNCAPPEDMaximize breadth

Predictable access where inventory growth matters more than scarcity.

04 · Migration dashboard

A pricing redesign is also a transition problem

Cohort modeling and pilots protect revenue while testing whether the new mechanic actually changes dealer behavior.

The commercial model should make full inventory economically rational, then monetize differentiated performance above it.
