CASE 06 / 06

Closing the Listings Gap

Commercial-model and product-architecture redesign for a challenger automotive marketplace whose pricing mechanics were unintentionally teaching dealers to under-list inventory.

The listings gap was not primarily a sales problem. It was an incentive-design problem: the marketplace wanted more stock while its commercial model charged dealers to display less of it.

01 · The diagnosis

A capped model can send full inventory to the competitor

The key behavioural insight was simple. If a dealer holds 120 vehicles but a package permits only 40 simultaneous listings, rotating the freshest or easiest-to-sell cars through those slots is rational. The full inventory naturally goes to the platform where breadth is less constrained.

WHAT THE MARKETPLACE WANTS

More inventory

More consumer choice → stronger search depth → more traffic → more leads → greater dealer value.

≠
WHAT THE MODEL REWARDS

Scarcity

Buy a limited number of slots → select hero stock → rotate it quickly → keep the rest somewhere else.

The core paradox: the platform was effectively asking dealers to pay for a constraint it did not strategically want to impose.
02 · Why rotation matters

A liquid market makes the slot mechanic even more powerful

In the source engagement, strong vehicles could sell in roughly two to three weeks. That means a dealer can reuse a limited slot more than once in a month — making selective rotation economically attractive while reducing the marketplace's visible breadth.

DEALER LOGICOptimise the slot

Put the most saleable inventory into the constrained channel and replace it as soon as it sells.

CONSUMER EFFECTLess market breadth

The marketplace sees a curated subset rather than the dealer's true stock, weakening choice and long-tail search coverage.

COMMERCIAL EFFECTValue shifts elsewhere

The uncapped competitor becomes the dealer's inventory system of record while the challenger risks becoming a secondary shop window.

03 · Reframe the commercial question

From “How do we sell more slots?” to “How do we reward breadth?”

The strategy was not to make listings free or abandon monetisation. It was to stop using inventory scarcity as the primary value meter and move revenue toward the outcomes and tools dealers are willing to pay more for.

BASE LAYER

Price participation

Stock-band, average-active-stock or uncapped structures make it rational to load the full book.

PERFORMANCE LAYER

Monetise visibility

Featured placement, premium exposure, bidding or lead products remain optional upside above broad inventory.

TOOLING LAYER

Monetise dealer value

Pricing intelligence, inventory tools and AI media create workflow value beyond advertising.

TRANSACTION LAYER

Monetise adjacent outcomes

Finance, insurance, history and selected transaction services reduce the need for the listing fee to carry all margin.

04 · International playbook

Different markets solve the same incentive problem in different ways

The benchmark was not about copying one foreign marketplace. It was about identifying mechanics that encourage inventory breadth while preserving multiple monetisation layers.

05 · Target portfolio

Breadth-friendly at the base. Value-rich as the dealer grows.

A redesigned portfolio should make participation rational for the long tail while preserving sophisticated commercial layers for larger dealers and groups.

ENTRY

Independent

Low-friction participation designed to capture small dealers currently defaulting to free alternatives.

CORE

SME dealer

Whole-stock / stock-band proposition with predictable monthly economics and essential lead tools.

GROWTH

Performance

Visibility boosts, AI media, dealer intelligence and enhanced lead products layered above inventory breadth.

ENTERPRISE

Dealer groups

Group-level commercial terms, reporting, integrations, data products and strategic services.

06 · Why the long tail matters

Small and mid-sized dealers are not a side segment — they improve marketplace economics

The source work treated the long tail as strategically important for more than revenue. A broader dealer base improves supply diversity, distributes leads more widely and reduces dependence on a handful of large groups with greater negotiating power.

Inventory breadth

More independent stock produces a more representative view of supply and more reasons for consumers to search the marketplace.

Revenue resilience

A wider paying base reduces concentration risk and dependence on a small number of enterprise accounts.

Lead distribution

Broader dealer participation prevents search results and demand from becoming excessively concentrated among the largest groups.

Negotiating balance

A healthier long tail reduces the leverage that a few large customers can exert over marketplace pricing and product decisions.

07 · Monetise above breadth

Once the whole book is online, more products become more valuable

Removing listing scarcity does not mean giving up revenue. It expands the inventory surface on which premium products, tools and adjacent services can operate.

VISIBILITY

Featured + premium placement

Let dealers pay to accelerate selected vehicles without forcing them to suppress the rest of their stock.

AI MEDIA

Automated inventory video

Bundle scalable vehicle creative into higher tiers to improve presentation and create product-led stickiness.

DEALER INTELLIGENCE

Pricing + demand insight

Integrate trusted inventory-management and live market-pricing capability so the marketplace becomes an operating tool, not only a shop window.

ADJACENCIES

F&I + history + transactions

Add revenue streams whose economics are tied to dealer and consumer outcomes rather than to restricting inventory.

Strategic flywheel: more dealer stock → more consumer choice → more marketplace data → better pricing and demand intelligence → more dealer utility → lower churn → more monetisable inventory.
08 · Revenue vs. listings

The redesign requires modelling the trade-off — not pretending it does not exist

Maximising listings and maximising short-term subscription revenue are not automatically the same objective. The engagement therefore called for 2–3 pricing scenarios and sensitivity analysis before committing to a migration.

SCENARIO AStock-band subscription

Price the dealer by total or average active inventory and improve effective per-unit economics as breadth grows.

SCENARIO BUncapped base + upsell

Use a predictable full-stock fee, then monetise premium visibility, tooling and adjacent services.

SCENARIO CEntry ladder

Create a lower-friction route for independents, then use performance products and tools to drive ARPA over time.

Decision test: compare each scenario on listings gained, ARPA, churn risk, dealer adoption, upsell potential and 12-month revenue — not on headline package price alone.
09 · Migration architecture

A better model can still fail if the transition damages the existing book

Commercial redesign had to respect live contracts, renewal cycles and dealer expectations. The migration was therefore designed cohort by cohort rather than as an overnight rate-card replacement.

COHORTS

Segment before migrating

Separate independents, SMEs, enterprise groups, high-rotation users and competitor-heavy dealers.

CONTRACTS

Honour renewal logic

Use renewal dates, grandfathering and controlled price changes rather than creating unnecessary commercial shock.

GUARDRAILS

Protect ARPA + churn

Track revenue leakage, adoption and churn risk as dealers move to the new mechanic.

ENABLEMENT

Give sales the story

Dealer communication, objection handling and tier-transition scripts are part of the product launch, not an afterthought.

10 · 10–12 week execution plan

Diagnose → redesign → transition → activate

WEEKS 1–3

Diagnostic & data

Audit rate card, tiers, slot caps and contracts; segment dealers; quantify matched-dealer inventory gaps; interview dealers and internal teams.

WEEKS 4–6

Model & portfolio

Design entry-to-enterprise portfolio; move toward breadth-rewarding mechanics; layer visibility, tooling and ancillary products; model 2–3 scenarios.

WEEKS 7–9

Transition plan

Build cohort migration, grandfathering, churn/ARPA guardrails, win-back motion, small-dealer acquisition and sales enablement.

WEEKS 10–12

Recommend & activate

Board-ready recommendation, 12-month impact model, Top-10 first-90-days actions and a pilot on one dealer cohort.

12 · Strategic conclusions

Five principles behind the redesign

Fix incentives first

If rational dealer behaviour produces the wrong marketplace outcome, redesign the mechanic before blaming sales execution.

Breadth before scarcity

The base product should encourage the marketplace to see the dealer's whole book; monetise premium outcomes above it.

Protect the long tail

Small and mid-sized dealers improve inventory, revenue diversification and negotiating balance.

Become operational

Pricing intelligence, inventory management and AI media can make the platform useful every day and reduce churn.

Migrate, don’t detonate

A commercially superior model still needs contract-aware sequencing, scenario modelling and dealer communication.

13 · Management takeaway

Do not obsess about defeating a distant market leader. Build a marketplace that is consistently better than its previous version.

Use pricing to encourage the inventory you want, use tools and data to become more valuable to dealers, and use the resulting breadth to improve the consumer proposition. The objective is not imitation. It is a stronger marketplace flywheel built around your own data, inventory and customer relationships.
