SANITIZED PORTFOLIO CASE · 03

PropTech AI — LATAM Market Development

A regional expansion framework translating a broad Latin American opportunity into sequenced markets, channels, partnerships and measurable tests.

A regional opportunity becomes manageable when market size is separated from market accessibility and distribution leverage.

01 · Market opportunity

Size the opportunity, then deliberately sanitize the forecast.

The original engagement combined country-level agent and listing estimates with adoption and unit-economics assumptions. For this public reconstruction, client-specific market figures and commercial assumptions are intentionally removed; the decision architecture is preserved.

Demand base

Estimate addressable professionals and listing activity by market.

Usage model

Translate users into plausible monthly content or workflow volume.

Economics

Test price-per-use and partner-share assumptions through scenarios, not a single forecast.

02 · Country prioritization

Not every large market should launch first.

Countries are scored through a practical market-entry lens: addressable demand, portal/CRM concentration, competitive intensity, localization, payment friction and access to distribution partners.

Wave 1

Large or accessible markets with credible distribution partners and manageable localization.

Wave 2

Markets activated after early references reduce adoption friction.

Wave 3

Opportunistic markets served through regional partners rather than dedicated country teams.

03 · Channel architecture

Sell through the ecosystem, not around it.

The model prioritizes partnerships with portals, CRMs, franchise networks and other platforms already serving the target professional audience. This compresses acquisition cost and creates embedded product usage.

Portal channel

Bundle AI capability into an existing marketplace proposition.

CRM channel

Embed workflows where agents already manage listings and leads.

Network channel

Use franchises and professional groups to aggregate adoption.

04 · Pilot design

Replace a big launch with measurable market tests.

Each launch begins with a bounded partner pilot. The aim is to validate activation, recurring use and willingness to pay before investing in a local commercial structure.

Activation

How many invited professionals complete first use?

Retention

Does usage repeat after the initial trial?

Unit economics

Does partner-led acquisition support sustainable contribution?

Expansion trigger

Scale only after product and distribution evidence cross agreed thresholds.

05 · 90-day execution

Sequence evidence before infrastructure.

The management cadence moves from partner validation to pilots and then to a regional scale decision.

Days 1–30

Validate assumptions, shortlist partners and localize the core proposition.

Days 31–60

Launch first partner tests and instrument adoption.

Days 61–90

Compare markets, refine economics and choose the next expansion wave.
