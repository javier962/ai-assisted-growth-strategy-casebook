02 · The management problem

Market size alone does not tell you where to launch

The strategy combined addressable demand with the practical ability to reach customers. A large agent population is attractive only if the company can build distribution, integrate into existing workflows and acquire customers at economics that justify the effort.

SIZEHow much demand exists?

Agent population, listing volume and realistic product usage.

ACCESSHow do we reach it?

Portals, CRMs, franchise groups, agent networks and direct channels.

ECONOMICSCan it scale?

Adoption assumptions, listing volumes, revenue share and cost-to-develop the market.

03 · Distribution architecture

Do not build the region one agent at a time

Distribution was treated as a strategic asset. The plan mapped the organisations already aggregating real-estate professionals and inventory so that one commercial relationship could accelerate adoption across a much larger customer base.

PROPERTY PORTALS

Marketplace distribution

Integrate or partner with major portals that already serve large populations of professional advertisers and can package AI capabilities into existing customer relationships.

CRM / SOFTWARE

Workflow distribution

Embed AI tools inside the systems agents already use for listings, marketing and lead management.

FRANCHISE GROUPS

Network rollout

Use franchise and multi-office groups to create concentrated adoption and operational proof across multiple branches.

DIRECT SALES

Selective customer acquisition

Use direct acquisition where it creates references, validates willingness to pay or fills gaps not covered by platform partners.

04 · Market sequencing

Wave 1 for proof. Wave 2 for extension.

The source plan placed Brazil, Colombia and Argentina into the initial launch wave, followed by Uruguay, Panama and Costa Rica from Month 4. The point was not simply to rank countries: it was to concentrate early execution, create evidence and then widen the footprint.

WAVE 1Brazil

Largest addressable base and the anchor market in the regional model.

WAVE 1Colombia + Argentina

Meaningful professional populations with enough scale to test regional repeatability.

WAVE 2 · M4Uruguay + Panama

WAVE 2 · M4Costa Rica

Smaller addressable base but useful for extending the regional motion.

06 · What the scenarios were testing

Forecasting was a decision tool, not a promise

The value of the model was not the precision of a 36-month number. It was making management assumptions explicit and showing how adoption speed changes the economics of expansion.

ADOPTION

How quickly do listings activate?

Small changes in partner conversion and customer adoption compound materially over a three-year horizon.

DISTRIBUTION

Do partnerships accelerate reach?

The model tests whether portals, CRMs and networks can move the business beyond a purely direct-sales acquisition curve.

PAYBACK

How long can the ramp be funded?

The conservative case exposes how long cash can remain negative when adoption is delayed.

SCALING

When does the region earn more investment?

Expansion capital should follow demonstrated distribution and adoption rather than precede it.

08 · Pilot scorecard

Expansion should earn the right to scale

A three-month pilot period was designed around mutually agreed commercial milestones. The public version removes confidential compensation terms but preserves the operating logic of the test.

Target outreach

Is the team reaching the priority portals, CRMs, franchise groups and channel partners identified in the plan?

Qualified meetings

Are introductions converting into conversations with decision-makers rather than simply increasing activity volume?

Commercial intent

Are conversations progressing toward pilots, LOIs, integrations or signed commercial agreements?

Adoption evidence

Do early customers and partners support the listing-volume and revenue assumptions behind the business plan?

09 · Management takeaway

International expansion should earn the right to scale.

Prioritise markets, build distribution, test commercial evidence and increase investment where the model proves itself. The scenario model then translates those operating choices into their financial consequences.
