SANITIZED PORTFOLIO CASE · 01

Automotive Data Intelligence

International go-to-market and partnership architecture for a specialist pricing and valuation technology platform.

Win as the intelligence layer inside existing marketplace and dealer workflows — not as another standalone valuation destination.

01 · Strategic diagnosis

The problem was not simply “enter more countries.”

A specialist automotive-data product needed a repeatable route into markets where valuation references, dealer software and marketplace economics differ materially. The strategic task was to distinguish where to compete, where to integrate and where to partner.

Market fit

Map incumbent valuation references and in-house marketplace tools before committing sales effort.

Distribution

Use portals as the high-leverage route to dealer audiences while opening workflow integrations in parallel.

Positioning

Lead with stock-level intelligence, sourcing and workflow value rather than a generic price-guide claim.

02 · Dual-track GTM

Marketplace reach + workflow stickiness.

The proposed architecture runs two channels in parallel: marketplace partnerships for reach and credibility, and DMS/CRM or dealer-group integrations for embedded recurring usage. This reduces dependence on a single enterprise sales cycle.

Track A · Marketplaces

Co-branded or white-label intelligence embedded in a portal’s dealer proposition.

Track B · Dealer systems

Data embedded in the software dealers already use, creating stickier recurring adoption.

Adjacency revenue

Extend the data layer toward finance, insurance, warranty and institutional valuation use cases.

03 · Market sequencing

Prioritize openness, economics and route-to-market — not geography alone.

Markets were screened for competitive intensity, local reference data, localization burden, dealer economics and partnership access. The output is a sequence of testable market entries rather than a broad international launch.

Open markets

Prioritize markets without a strong in-house live-pricing tool.

Integrate where needed

Where a local reference is entrenched, complement it rather than trying to replace it.

Localize selectively

Language, currency and privacy requirements become explicit gates in the rollout.

04 · Execution roadmap

Turn the thesis into a 90-day commercial experiment.

The first phase is deliberately designed as a validation sprint: learn the product and pipeline, build the prioritized action list, map partner routes, start outreach, then review traction before scaling.

Weeks 1–2

Onboarding, product economics, existing integrations and pipeline.

Weeks 2–5

Prioritized GTM plan and Top Actions; market-by-market entry logic.

Days 20–60

Targeted outreach and qualified partner conversations.

Month 2–3

Co-develop partner opportunities; review evidence and decide where to scale.

05 · Risk architecture

Make the constraints part of the strategy.

The plan explicitly accounts for long portal cycles, entrenched references, localization, privacy and the risk of partners building internally.

Sales-cycle hedge

Run embedded distribution in parallel while larger portal deals mature.

Competitive hedge

Win on workflow and stock intelligence, not headline valuation alone.

Build-vs-partner

Use preferred integration and switching-cost logic where it strengthens defensibility.
