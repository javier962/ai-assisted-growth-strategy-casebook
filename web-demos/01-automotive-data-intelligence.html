01 · The business challenge

International expansion was not a country-selection exercise

The company had a specialist product combining live stock intelligence, pricing and valuation capabilities for automotive retailers. The strategic question was not simply “which countries are largest?” It was where the product could occupy a defensible position in an already crowded automotive technology stack — and whether entry should happen by competing, integrating or partnering.

COMMERCIAL PROBLEM

Direct dealer sales do not scale equally everywhere

Dealer fragmentation, local software ecosystems and different willingness to pay make a single international sales playbook inefficient.

STRATEGIC CONSTRAINT

Valuation references are deeply local

Many markets already have a trusted book-value or pricing reference. Replacing it head-on creates unnecessary resistance.

DISTRIBUTION OPPORTUNITY

Portals already aggregate dealer demand

A marketplace can distribute the intelligence layer to hundreds or thousands of dealers through one enterprise relationship.

WORKFLOW OPPORTUNITY

DMS / CRM platforms sit inside daily operations

Embedding pricing and stock intelligence where dealers already work can create recurring usage and switching cost.

02 · Market-entry framework

Compete, integrate or partner?

Each market was assessed not only for size but for ecosystem structure: incumbent valuation references, marketplace openness, dealer-system access, localisation burden and vehicle economics.

COMPETEWhere the data gap is real

Lead with live stock-level pricing, sourcing intelligence and measurable inventory outcomes where incumbent tools leave a clear gap.

INTEGRATEWhere the reference is entrenched

Benchmark against the trusted local reference, then add workflow intelligence rather than forcing replacement.

PARTNERWhere distribution is concentrated

Use portals, DMS platforms and dealer groups as multipliers instead of building dealer-by-dealer sales from zero.

Core positioning: win as a data layer — not as the headline valuation guide. Differentiate through live stock, sourcing and workflow intelligence.

04 · The GTM architecture

Two distribution tracks — run at the same time

Large marketplace deals create reach and credibility but can take months. DMS / CRM integrations can reach revenue sooner and become sticky once embedded. Running both was designed as a cash-flow hedge.

TRACK A · MARKETPLACES

White-label / co-branded intelligence

Supply real-time stock-level pricing and valuation inside the portal’s dealer proposition. One enterprise relationship can distribute the capability across a large dealer base.

TRACK B · DMS / CRM

API and workflow integration

Embed pricing, valuation and stock intelligence into systems dealers already use. A platform integration can unlock multiple dealer groups and markets.

05 · Revenue architecture

Dealer SaaS was only one revenue pool

The same underlying data capability could support several customer types and commercial models.

Portal licensing

White-label or co-branded dealer intelligence distributed through marketplace products.

DMS data layer

API / integration revenue with recurring usage embedded in dealer workflows.

OEM & fleet

Stock, pricing and market intelligence for distribution, remarketing and network decisions.

Finance & insurance

Real-time valuation feeds for LTV, underwriting, loan origination and claims decisions.

“Invisible customer” opportunity: banks and insurers can consume valuation intelligence without dealer adoption being the gating factor, creating a different recurring B2B data revenue stream.

07 · Commercial value proposition

Sell inventory economics, not software features

The commercial proposition was reframed around measurable dealer outcomes. Every unsold day ties up working capital and increases inventory risk, making days-in-stock a more useful opening conversation than a feature list.

01

Days-in-stock

Use better pricing and sourcing decisions to accelerate stock turn.

02

Gross profit / unit

Balance faster rotation with disciplined margin management.

03

Pricing accuracy

Use live evidence to identify vehicles positioned above or below competitive stock.

04

Sourcing quality

Identify which vehicles are more likely to sell before capital is committed.

Pricing cannot be one-size-fits-all. The source analysis identified roughly a threefold spread in new-vehicle transaction values between lower- and higher-value markets, affecting inventory economics and willingness to pay for decision intelligence.
08 · Risk architecture

Make the constraints part of the GTM design

Risks were used to determine sequencing, channel mix and where not to over-invest.

SALES CYCLEEnterprise portal deals can take months

Keep DMS / CRM integration and smaller proof points moving while strategic negotiations mature.

INCUMBENTSTrusted local references are hard to displace

Integrate or benchmark against them; differentiate on live stock and workflow intelligence.

LOCALISATIONLanguage is only one layer

Currency, vehicle taxonomy, data coverage, privacy requirements and pricing behaviour become rollout gates.

BUILD vs PARTNERLarge platforms may build internally

Make integration faster and economically more attractive than recreating the capability in-house.

ECONOMICSWillingness to pay differs materially

Calibrate pricing and packaging to local dealer economics instead of exporting one global price sheet.

FOCUSToo many countries dilute execution

Scale only where partner access, economics and product fit are demonstrated.

09 · Strategic conclusions

Four choices shaped the expansion plan

Distribution before geography

A smaller market with an anchor channel can be more attractive than a larger market requiring a cold dealer-by-dealer build.

Data layer before destination

Fit into the ecosystem instead of forcing customers to adopt another standalone valuation destination.

Two tracks, not one

Use portal partnerships for scale and DMS integrations for embedded recurring usage.

Monetise the data broadly

The same intelligence can serve dealer workflow and institutional valuation use cases with different economics.

10 · Management takeaway

International growth is a distribution-design problem before it is a country-count problem.

The strategic shift was from “sell the product in more countries” to “identify where the product can become an intelligence layer inside an existing ecosystem, prove the economics, and scale through distribution partners.”
